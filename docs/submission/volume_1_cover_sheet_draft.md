## Section 5: Technical Abstract (Public Release)

*Copy/paste the text below into the DSIP "Technical Abstract" text box. This text is non-proprietary and is approved for public release.*

> FrontierGrid-Observant is an unclassified Phase I feasibility testbed for risk-aware regenerative AI-based multimodal visual-tactical monitoring, understanding, alerting, and assistance. Naval mission environments produce fragmented, time-sensitive data across sensors, operators, networks, documents, and autonomous systems. Existing centralized AI orchestration can become brittle when events are incomplete, contradictory, degraded, delayed, or distributed across domains.
> 
> Techman Studios proposes to investigate a federated contextual-state approach that adapts prior SOL, Frontier_OS, and Exciton-MoA research into an Observant-AI-style prototype. The Phase I effort will build at least three synthetic cross-domain challenge problems, a common event schema, lightweight mission-focused agent roles, a deterministic fallback reasoning substrate, risk scoring and escalation controls, replayable explanations, and a test-and-evaluation harness. The current testbed includes five synthetic scenarios whose anomaly evidence rotates across Sea, Space, Air, Land, and Cyber style domains, including an unknown-unknown injection case that introduces a weak-signal disturbance into the continuous state-space stream. The prototype will compare shared-context behavior against rule-based and centralized baselines using sensitivity, specificity, precision, miss rate, false discovery rate, false omission rate, analytic completeness, uniqueness, validity, consistency, accuracy under noisy data, context-geodesic alignment, active-domain coverage, contribution balance, and candidate-team completeness.
> 
> The expected result is a controlled feasibility demonstration showing whether multi-agent contextual fusion can improve risk-aware alerting and source-grounded explanation without requiring classified data in Phase I. Dual-use applications include maritime logistics anomaly detection, critical infrastructure monitoring, emergency response situational awareness, and enterprise risk intelligence.

## Section 6: Anticipated Benefits & Commercialization (Public Release)

*Copy/paste the text below into the DSIP "Anticipated Benefits" text box. This text is non-proprietary and is approved for public release.*

> **Naval Benefits:**
> The primary benefit to the Navy is earlier warning and reduced false-discovery rates when dealing with ambiguous cross-domain event clusters (such as maritime shadowing or littoral navigational hazards). By structuring event interpretation on a shared contextual state-space, the system separates event risk from data-quality risk, provides explicit skeptic-agent reviews, and produces source-grounded natural-language explanations. This reduces operator cognitive load and enhances trust during degraded communication states or decoy behaviors.
> 
> **Technical Benefits:**
> The prototype implements a modular adapter-first architecture that keeps a deterministic baseline active while allowing controlled experiments with native manifold-based agent coordination. It provides standard JSON schemas for multi-modal track, network, weather, and text inputs, paired with a reproducible testing and evaluation metrics sweep.
> 
> **Dual-Use Commercial Applications:**
> Commercial applications include critical infrastructure monitoring, maritime cargo/logistics risk analysis, emergency management situational awareness, and enterprise operations monitoring. These commercial sectors face similar challenges: sparse observations, delayed report streams, conflicting source reliability, and a critical need for explainable escalation over black-box scoring.
